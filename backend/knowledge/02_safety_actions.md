# Safety actions for thunderstorm hazards

## Lightning safety
- "When thunder roars, go indoors." If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck.
- Shelter in a solid building or a hard-topped vehicle with the windows closed. Huts, open sheds, tents and bus shelters do not protect you.
- Stay indoors until about 30 minutes after the last thunder.
- Indoors, avoid corded phones, plumbing and electrical appliances, and unplug sensitive electronics.
- In the open with no shelter: keep away from tall isolated trees, poles, towers, water bodies and metal fences. Crouch low on the balls of your feet, head down, feet together. Do not lie flat.
- Groups in the open should spread out several metres apart.
- Farmers should stop field work, leave tractors and open fields, and bring livestock away from lone trees.
- In India, the Damini lightning app (IITM/Ministry of Earth Sciences) gives lightning alerts near your location.

## Hail safety
- Move people, vehicles and livestock under sturdy cover.
- Stay away from windows and skylights.
- Farmers: cover nursery beds and high-value crops with hail nets where available, and harvest ripe crops early if a hail warning is issued.
- After hail, watch for slippery surfaces and damaged roofs.

## Squalls and downbursts (damaging winds)
- Downbursts can produce sudden gusts above 80–100 km/h and flatten crops, trees, hoardings and weak structures within minutes.
- Secure loose objects, stay indoors away from windows, and do not shelter under trees, hoardings or weak walls.
- Drivers should slow down, and two-wheelers should stop in a safe place.
- Aviation: downbursts cause dangerous low-level wind shear on approach and departure. Airports should consider holding or diverting when a downburst or strong-gust threat is forecast within about 10 km.

## Cloudbursts and flash floods
- A cloudburst can drop 100 mm or more of rain in an hour over a small area, causing flash floods and landslides, especially in hills (Himalayas, Meghalaya, Western Ghats).
- Move to higher ground, and do not cross flowing streams or flooded roads on foot or by vehicle.
- In hilly areas, avoid slopes, riverbanks and landslide-prone roads during and after very heavy rain.
- Keep an emergency kit ready (torch, phone power bank, drinking water, medicines, documents).

## What to do at each threat level
- **Low**: stay alert and check the forecast before outdoor work or travel.
- **Moderate**: plan to be indoors when the countdown ends; secure loose items; farmers should protect harvested produce.
- **High**: move indoors before arrival; stop outdoor work, sport and gatherings; drivers should pull over during the peak.
- **Extreme**: act immediately and take the strongest shelter available. Authorities should issue public warnings and ready response teams.

## Emergency contacts in India
- National emergency number: 112.
- Disaster management helpline: 1078 (NDMA / state control rooms).
- Follow official warnings from IMD (mausam.imd.gov.in) and your State Disaster Management Authority.
